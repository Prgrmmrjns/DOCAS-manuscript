\begin{abstract}
We present a practical workflow for steering model behavior with lightweight structural rules while preserving predictive performance.
The key idea is to iteratively generate synthetic training points and optimize them against a combined objective:
held-out model performance (RMSE) and a SHAP-derived feasibility score that measures alignment with user-defined directional rules.
Unlike post-hoc explanation-only methods, our approach actively edits training data to improve causal plausibility of learned effects.
We provide an implementation based on LightGBM, Optuna, and TreeSHAP, and demonstrate the pipeline on two tabular datasets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Many machine learning systems achieve strong predictive metrics but violate simple domain expectations.
For example, in glucose forecasting one may expect that increased insulin should decrease future glucose, yet unconstrained models may learn the opposite or unstable directions.
This creates a gap between pure predictive optimization and rule-consistent behavior.

We propose iSHAP, a method that uses structural rules and SHAP-based diagnostics to guide synthetic augmentation.
At each round, candidate synthetic points are optimized with respect to a combined objective balancing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item prediction quality on hold-out data, and
    \item rule feasibility measured from SHAP directional correlations.
\end{enumerate}
If a candidate improves the combined objective, it is added to training data and the model is retrained.
This creates a closed loop between model fit, explanation diagnostics, and data augmentation.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Problem Setup}
Given training data $(X, y)$ and a set of directed rules
$\mathcal{R} = \{(u \rightarrow v, s)\}$ with desired sign $s \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$,
we train a base model and iteratively add synthetic points.
For each rule, feasibility is computed by correlating SHAP effects with corresponding features.
For target rules $(u \rightarrow y)$, we use
\[
\rho_u = \mathrm{corr}\big(\phi_u(x), x_u\big),
\]
and compare $\rho_u$ to target sign $s$.
Aggregating weighted deviations across rules yields a feasibility score in $[0,1]$.

\subsection{Optimization Objective}
At each round, Optuna (TPE sampler) proposes synthetic points.
Each trial is scored by a combined objective that rewards lower RMSE and higher feasibility.
The trade-off is controlled by \texttt{objective\_rmse\_weight}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{0.0}: prioritize RMSE only,
    \item \texttt{1.0}: prioritize feasibility only,
    \item intermediate values: linear trade-off.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Round-Based Augmentation}
The pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Train baseline model on current augmented set.
    \item Optimize a batch of synthetic points.
    \item Evaluate combined score on fixed validation diagnostics.
    \item Accept batch if combined score improves; otherwise stop.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Datasets}
We currently evaluate:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{d1namo}: glucose-related tabular regression task.
    \item \textbf{cancer\_air\_pollution}: cancer-level dataset with air-pollution risk factors.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Rules}
For controlled sanity checks, we use one rule per dataset:
\begin{itemize}
    \item d1namo: \texttt{insulin} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{future glucose}, negative.
    \item cancer\_air\_pollution: \texttt{Air Pollution} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{Level}, positive.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}
\subsection{Quantitative Summary}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} summarizes the current runs.
For both datasets, the approach improves feasibility and hold-out RMSE, indicating that rule alignment can be increased without sacrificing predictive quality in these initial experiments.

\begin{table}[ht]
    \centering
    \caption{Before/after results from current pipeline runs.}
    \label{tab:main_results}
    \begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
        \toprule
        Dataset & $\Delta$ Feasibility & RMSE Before & RMSE After & Synth Rows Added \\
        \midrule
        d1namo & +0.0995 & 28.3282 & 28.0208 & 30 \\
        cancer\_air\_pollution & +0.0025 & 0.0066 & 0.0023 & 10 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

For d1namo, rule alignment improved substantially:
\texttt{insulin $\rightarrow$ future glucose} moved from Pearson $-0.4299$ to $-0.6289$, closer to the desired negative direction.
At the same time, RMSE improved by $-0.3074$.
For cancer\_air\_pollution, the directional rule \texttt{Air Pollution $\rightarrow$ Level} improved from $0.6612$ to $0.6661$, while RMSE improved from $0.0066$ to $0.0023$.

\section{Discussion}
These initial results support the central premise of iSHAP: synthetic augmentation can move a model toward predefined directional rules while preserving or improving predictive performance.
The gains are strongest in d1namo, where the rule conflict was larger at baseline and thus allowed more directional correction.
The cancer dataset shows a smaller feasibility gain, likely because baseline alignment was already relatively high.

A practical benefit is interpretability of progress at each round:
the feasibility metric, per-rule Pearson shifts, and explanation plots all move in a coherent direction.
This makes the optimization process auditable, rather than relying only on aggregate predictive metrics.

Overall, the current evidence suggests that rule-guided augmentation is a viable mechanism to reduce explanation--domain mismatch in tabular settings.
Further validation across additional datasets and richer SCM rule sets is needed to establish robustness.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
Current limitations include potential search inefficiency in high-dimensional spaces and dependence on SHAP stability.
Future work should investigate stronger structural constraints, alternative attribution methods, and more robust optimization schemes.

\section{Conclusion}
iSHAP offers a practical framework for injecting domain rules into model development through data-centric optimization.
By coupling synthetic augmentation with explanation-based feasibility scoring, practitioners can iteratively move models toward both stronger performance and more plausible directional behavior.
