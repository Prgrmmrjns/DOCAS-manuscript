\begin{abstract}
We present a practical workflow for steering model behavior with lightweight structural rules while preserving predictive performance.
The key idea is to iteratively generate synthetic training points and optimize them against a combined objective:
hold-out task performance (root mean squared error for regression, accuracy for multi-class classification) and a feasibility score grounded in \emph{do}-interventions on the fitted model, not correlation with SHAP values alone.
Unlike post-hoc explanation-only methods, our approach actively edits training data to align predicted interventional effects with user-specified directional rules.
We provide an implementation based on LightGBM, Optuna (TPE), and TreeSHAP only for optional plots, and demonstrate the pipeline on two tabular datasets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Many machine learning systems achieve strong predictive metrics but violate simple domain expectations.
For example, in glucose forecasting one may expect that increased insulin should decrease future glucose, yet unconstrained models may learn unstable or non-monotone interventional curves.
This creates a gap between pure predictive optimization and rule-consistent behavior.

We propose iSHAP, a method that uses structural rules and model-based \emph{do}-effects to guide synthetic augmentation.
At each round, candidate synthetic points are optimized with respect to a combined objective balancing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item prediction quality on hold-out data (task-appropriate metric), and
    \item rule feasibility from interventional summaries of the model response.
\end{enumerate}
If a candidate improves the combined objective, it is added to training data and the model is retrained.
This creates a closed loop between model fit, interventional diagnostics, and data augmentation.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Problem Setup}
Given training data $(X, y)$ and directed rules
$\mathcal{R} = \{(u \rightarrow v, s)\}$ with desired qualitative sign $s \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ (negative, none, positive),
we train a predictive model and evaluate rule consistency under interventions implied by the model map.

\paragraph{Rules to the target.}
For a rule $(u \rightarrow y)$, fix a validation covariate matrix $X$ and sweep a grid $g$ over the observed range of feature $u$.
Define the interventional prediction curve
\[
  \bar{f}(g) \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n f\big(X^{(i)}_{\neg u},\, u{=}g\big),
\]
where row $i$ is copied and only column $u$ is set to $g$.
We summarize alignment with the desired monotone direction (higher $u$ should raise or lower $\bar{f}$ depending on $s$) using a monotone score derived from the consecutive differences of $\bar{f}(g)$ along the grid (full agreement with the desired direction yields the strongest feasibility contribution).
A Pearson correlation between $g$ and $\bar{f}(g)$ is retained as a diagnostic correlate of global slope but feasibility optimization emphasizes monotone agreement with the rule sign.

\paragraph{Rules between features.}
For $(u \rightarrow v)$ with both $u,v$ among inputs, we use the partial correlation between $X_u$ and $X_v$ after linearly controlling for all other observed features---a compact backdoor-style adjustment when the full causal graph is unknown.

Aggregating weighted deviations of each rule's summary from the ideal sign yields a feasibility score in $[0,1]$.

\subsection{Optimization Objective}
At each round, Optuna (TPE sampler) proposes synthetic points.
Each trial is scored by a combined objective that rewards better hold-out performance and higher feasibility.
The trade-off is controlled by \texttt{objective\_metric\_weight} in code:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{0.0}: prioritize hold-out performance only (regression: lower root mean squared error; classification: higher accuracy),
    \item \texttt{1.0}: prioritize feasibility only,
    \item intermediate values: linear trade-off in the implemented scalar.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Round-Based Augmentation}
The pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Train baseline model on current augmented set.
    \item Optimize a batch of synthetic points.
    \item Evaluate combined score on fixed validation diagnostics.
    \item Accept batch if combined score improves; otherwise stop.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Datasets}
We currently evaluate:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{d1namo}: glucose-related tabular \emph{regression}; hold-out metric is root mean squared prediction error (reported as \texttt{error} in outputs).
    \item \textbf{cancer\_air\_pollution}: cancer risk \emph{classification} with ordinal labels mapped to classes; hold-out metric is classification \emph{accuracy}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Rules}
For controlled sanity checks, we use one rule per dataset:
\begin{itemize}
    \item d1namo: \texttt{insulin} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{future glucose}, negative (higher insulin should correspond to lower predicted future glucose along the do-curve).
    \item cancer\_air\_pollution: \texttt{Air Pollution} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{Level}, positive.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
Interventional feasibility makes the notion of ``insulin lowers future glucose'' directly testable on the fitted predictor, independently of beeswarm correlations.
Splitting hold-out metrics by task (RMSE vs.\ accuracy) avoids mis-stating performance on the cancer benchmark.
The stored do-curve tables allow external reproduction and alternative plot styles without re-fitting.

Current limitations include dependence on the choice of validation rows for the do-sweep, grid resolution, and the strength of partial-correlation surrogates for non-target rules when the true graph is unknown.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
Future work should investigate richer SCM-based adjustment sets for feature--feature rules, adaptive grids, and joint multi-rule interventional constraints.

\section{Conclusion}
iSHAP couples data-centric synthetic search with interventional and partial-correlation feasibility so practitioners can trade predictive metrics against rule-shaped model behavior with auditable curves and saved numeric artifacts.
