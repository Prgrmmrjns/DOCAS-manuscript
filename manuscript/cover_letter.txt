Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript entitled

“Insulin Dose–Response Alignment for Glucose Forecasting in Type 1 Diabetes”

for publication in Artificial Intelligence in Medicine as an original methodological research contribution.

The mere application of a published learner to clinical logs is outside the journal’s scope. This manuscript is not that: it operationalizes a training-time method, DOCAS, that aligns a supervised model’s individual conditional expectation (ICE) on one treatment feature to a generic, population-level dose–response curve. It does not infer biology, learn an unknown DRC, or individualize the curve; that curve is supplied. Post-hoc XAI can diagnose a wrong-signed ICE; it does not repair it. Synthetic do-style rows are labelled with the incremental target effect and the same architecture is trained once on observational and synthetic data.

We test this on type 1 diabetes glucose forecasting, where the insulin response is known and a wrong-signed forecast is clinically dangerous. On OhioT1DM (n=12) and AZT1D (n=24), the fitted insulin response tracks the stated population DRC at a small held-out RMSE cost. In a ReplayBG corrective-bolus evaluation on open-loop OhioT1DM, the aligned model raises post-decision time in range from 73.61 to 76.01 percent with no time below range under an explicit hypoglycaemia guard.

We believe this fits the journal’s aim: a concrete AI methodology with a demonstrated medical decision-support case, rather than a routine application of an existing algorithm.
